# Lesson 17: Challenge Guide - Data Storytelling Dashboard
 
### Estimated Timing: 120 Minutes

## Overview

Welcome to the Data Storytelling Challenge. In this lab, you will analyze the goal, explore the data, and figure out how to build a clear and impactful dashboard on your own.

Your task is to use data, visuals, and interactivity to tell a story that helps decision-makers understand a problem and take action.

You will work like a real *data analyst* — asking questions, testing ideas, and refining your dashboard for clarity and impact.


## Your Mission

You are an analyst hired by a city council. They are concerned about how one of the following systems is performing:

- **Traffic** – congestion near schools and major roads
- **Water** – waste or inefficiencies in water usage
- **Energy** – spikes in electricity consumption

Your mission is to:

- Identify a problem using real data
- Uncover an insight using visuals, filters, or slicers
- Propose a clear recommendation based on your findings
- This story must be communicated through a Power BI dashboard.

## Challenge Objectives

You are expected to decide how to complete each step below. Use what you already know about Power BI, dashboards, and data analysis.

### 1. Choose a System & Dataset

Decide which system you want to investigate:

- Traffic
- Water
- Energy

Then find your own dataset from a reliable public source (preferably a .gov site).

**Your dataset should:**

- Be structured (CSV or similar)
- Have at least 100 rows
- Include multiple useful columns (date, location, value, category, etc.)

*Challenge yourself:* How do you know if a dataset is trustworthy and useful?


### 2. Import & Prepare the Data

Bring your dataset into Power BI and prepare it for analysis.

Think about:

- Are column names clear and easy to understand?
- Do any columns need to be renamed for clarity?
- Are there blanks or unnecessary rows?
- Are data types correct (numbers, dates, text)?

*Analyst mindset:* Clean data makes clear stories. Messy data leads to confusion.


### 3. Build the Dashboard

You must design a dashboard that tells a story, not just shows charts.
Your dashboard must include:

- At least 3 visuals
- At least 1 slicer
- Clear titles and labels
- A clean, readable layout

Ask yourself:

- Which visuals best explain the problem?
- What comparisons will help someone understand the issue quickly?
- How can a slicer help answer different questions without rebuilding the dashboard?

**Hint:** Decision-makers don’t want everything — they want what matters most.


### 4. Use Interactivity to Discover Insights

Use slicers, filters, or cross-filtering to interact with your dashboard.
Explore questions like:

- What changes when I filter by location or category?
- Are there patterns at certain times or places?
- What insight is revealed only after filtering the data?

Write down at least one insight that was not obvious before using slicers.


### 5. Tell the Story: Problem → Insight → Recommendation

Your dashboard should support a clear story.

Complete the following:
- **Problem:** What issue does the data reveal?
- **Insight:** What pattern or comparison helped you understand the problem more deeply?
- **Recommendation:** What action should be taken based on the data?

Your recommendation should be realistic and actionable.

Example:

- *Problem:* Evening energy use is too high.
- *Insight:* Usage spikes between 6–9 PM in residential areas.
- *Recommendation:* Offer incentives for off-peak appliance usage.


## Deliverables (What You Will Submit)

By the end of the challenge, you must submit:

1. Power BI Dashboard
- Minimum 3 visuals
- Minimum 1 slicer

2. Dataset source citation (link)

3. Written Recommendation (3–4 sentences) using the Problem → Insight → Recommendation model

## Success Criteria

**To complete this challenge successfully:**

- Verify that the Power BI dashboard is created with at least 3 visuals and 1 slicer, and that visuals respond correctly when slicers or filters are used.

- Ensure the data is populated correctly and reflects meaningful patterns or differences based on interaction (such as filtering by location, category, or time).

- Confirm that the dashboard clearly communicates a Problem → Insight → Recommendation flow that a city decision-maker can easily understand.

## Conclusion

This challenge is designed to simulate how data is used in the real world.

You were not asked to follow instructions — you were asked to think like an analyst, make design decisions, test ideas, and communicate meaning through data.

A strong dashboard is not about using every feature in Power BI. It is about clarity, insight, and action. If your dashboard helps a decision-maker quickly understand a problem and what to do about it, you have succeeded.

Use what you learned here as a foundation for future projects where data must inform real decisions.
